# SwarmCS

C++ ground control station for ArduPilot and PX4 multicopters.

Ctrl+click vehicle titles/cards or group headers to toggle membership in the current selection. A selection matching a complete saved group uses that group's controls; other multi-vehicle selections show a temporary **Selected** control card above the saved groups. Vehicles remain in their saved groups. A plain click replaces the selection.

**Plan** captures the selected vehicles and downloads their existing missions using the MAVLink mission protocol. **Fly** returns to flight controls. Planning cards show only names and firmware. Downloaded missions are displayed unchanged, including unsupported commands and altitude frames. SwarmCS-generated plans restore their editable center route when the downloaded missions match; other imported missions expose individual vehicle waypoints for dragging/deleting, with an explicit notice that swarm separation has not been checked. **Clear** clears missions on every planning vehicle, waits for acknowledgements, then starts a new draft with a 50 ft relative takeoff. Clear and Upload require connected, disarmed participants. Transfer failures identify the affected vehicles; success is shown only after all acknowledgements.

In a new draft, click the map to add center waypoints and edit relative altitude and incoming speed in the right panel. Units default to feet and mph; click units to switch altitude/separation to meters and speed to kph. Unit preferences persist. The thick cyan path follows the exact clicked centers; orange paths use route-relative lateral lanes, ordered by the vehicles' takeoff positions across the first leg. The generator adds vertical cruise lanes and transition items, checks minimum 3D separation over continuous nominal linear transitions, and returns to the original horizontal/vertical takeoff formation at the final waypoint. A final landing command is not inserted automatically. The displayed incoming speed is the desired maximum ground speed for that leg; generated per-vehicle ground/climb/descent speed commands and waits target a common nominal leg duration.

Routes are limited to a 25 km local planning area and 500 m relative altitude. A starting/landing formation already closer than the requested separation is rejected because preserving that formation cannot meet the constraint. Upload is disabled for unresolved routes. The check covers planned geometry and nominal timing, not terrain, obstacles, tracking error, or a live arrival barrier between independently executing autopilots. Use simulation to validate generated missions before flight. Mission exchange follows the [MAVLink mission protocol](https://mavlink.io/en/services/mission.html).

The map defaults to Esri World Imagery satellite tiles. The bottom-right button switches between satellite and OpenStreetMap street tiles while preserving center and zoom. Each layer uses a separate local tile cache. Maps require internet access and retain provider attribution.

The right-side zoom slider has 20 steps, from 1 (closest) at the top to 20 (farthest) at the bottom, with the selected number above it. It starts at 5 and remembers the selected level across restarts. Mouse-wheel scrolling and touchpad/touchscreen pinch gestures update the same zoom setting around the pointer or gesture center.

The map initially centers on the computer's location when available through Qt Positioning or the Windows location service. Without a position or permission it keeps the Indianapolis fallback. A first fix centers once; manually moving the map prevents a late fix from interrupting navigation. The Windows fallback reads available location reports for up to 15 seconds without changing system privacy settings.

The top gear opens a preview of connection options; those fields do not change the connection behavior yet. Connect binds IPv4 UDP port 14550 and discovers multicopters from valid MAVLink 1/2 heartbeats. While searching, the status cycles through zero to three dots. Known vehicles receive a GCS heartbeat once per second; they expire after five seconds without a heartbeat. Disconnect closes the socket. Bind failures appear in the top bar.

## Build

Install CMake 3.24+, a C++17 compiler, and Qt 6.8+ with Qt Quick, Quick Controls 2, Network, Location (including the OSM plugin), and Positioning. The initial configure downloads the official MAVLink C headers, so internet access and Git are required.

```powershell
cmake -S . -B build -DCMAKE_PREFIX_PATH="C:/Qt/6.8.3/msvc2022_64"
cmake --build build --config Debug
./build/bin/Debug/SwarmCS.exe
```

Use your installed Qt kit path and its matching compiler/runtime. On single-configuration generators the executable is directly in `build/bin/`. Deploy Qt libraries and QML plugins with your Qt kit's `windeployqt --qmldir . build/bin/Debug/SwarmCS.exe` when running outside the Qt development environment.

Run the UDP integration tests with `ctest --test-dir build -C Debug --output-on-failure`. Port 14550 must be free.

## Try discovery

Configure ArduPilot SITL, PX4 SITL, or your telemetry router to send MAVLink UDP to this computer's IP on port **14550**, then click **Connect**. UDP discovery listens for incoming traffic; it does not scan network addresses. Permit incoming UDP 14550 through the firewall, and close any other GCS using that port. The map needs internet access for tiles; OpenStreetMap attribution remains visible.

Connected vehicles appear in the left Vehicles tree, initially stacked under Ungrouped. Cards use a 75% scale with centered, evenly spaced values. Each shows its name, firmware type and reported version, armed state, active flight mode, altitude relative to home (metres), and horizontal ground speed labelled Velocity. The version is requested from the flight controller on discovery; until reported, only the firmware name appears. Left-click Velocity to switch every card between kph and mph; the unit preference is saved. Missing or stale telemetry displays a dash. SwarmCS requests position and ground-speed messages at 2 Hz when a vehicle is discovered.

Each card's gear opens vehicle settings. Names save as you type, with collisions automatically numbered in system-ID order (including saved offline identities). The input keeps the requested base name while the displayed name includes any necessary suffix. Icon choices also persist by sysID: ArduCopter/PX4 offer quadcopter, hexacopter, octocopter, and bullet drone silhouettes; ArduPlane offers fixed wing, delta wing, and wing silhouettes. A saved name labels each valid, fresh map position. Clicking the card centers on its position while preserving the selected zoom.

Right-click a vehicle for Arm/Disarm or the Add to group submenu. Names, icons, groups, and memberships are saved locally with Qt settings; vehicle identity uses the MAVLink system ID, which must be unique across connected vehicles. Right-click the Vehicles title to clear saved names, including offline vehicles, restoring default Vehicle N names. Icons and group assignments remain intact.

Click a card's flight mode to choose another mode. SwarmCS queries the MAVLink available-modes service and uses its complete mode list when supported, otherwise displaying firmware defaults. Unsupported or non-selectable modes cannot be forced. Requests use the firmware's ArduPilot/PX4 mode encoding, and arm/disarm always leaves force flags unset. Pending commands are confirmed by heartbeat/current-mode telemetry. Brief command feedback replaces Connected in the top bar, then returns to connection status after a few seconds. Flight commands do not open result dialogs. Only commands selected by the user are sent; no automatic arming or mode changes occur.

Each vehicle card has a small error label at the bottom, shown when needed. It displays autopilot warnings/errors, pre-arm reasons, command rejections and timeouts; hover for the full text. Status text is accepted only from that vehicle's discovered flight controller endpoint, including reassembly of multi-packet messages. Normal informational messages do not overwrite an error. Pre-arm errors clear when the vehicle reports healthy pre-arm checks or becomes armed. Other command errors clear on a successful command. SwarmCS requests pre-arm diagnostics on discovery and after a rejected arm request without changing or bypassing checks. Parameter-save result dialogs remain available for skipped-vehicle and failure reports.

Right-click a group header to arm or disarm its connected vehicles, start their existing missions, request RTL, change their flight mode together, or open Parameters. Group mode choices include modes shared by the connected members, resolved to each vehicle's own mode encoding. Mission start requires already-armed vehicles and waits for command acknowledgement. Group commands are individual requests; vehicles can independently reject them.

The Parameters popup initially selects the first connected group member. Its vehicle dropdown changes the viewed vehicle while preserving the search text and each vehicle's pending edits. Search filters parameter names as you type. Reload downloads a fresh table; edits remain pending until Save. Standard MAVLink numeric parameters are downloaded with missing-index retries; unsupported transport types remain read-only. Integer ranges and float-transport precision are checked before writes.

Parameter rows also show reference descriptions, units and valid ranges. Hover the parameter name for the full explanation and enumerated values or bit definitions. Bundled ArduPilot/PX4 reference snapshots work offline; firmware variants may differ, and missing metadata is explicitly marked. See `assets/parameters/README.md` for sources and refresh instructions.

The top bar's Takeoff altitude defaults to **50 ft**. Click its unit to toggle feet/meters without changing the actual height; both the height and unit persist locally. Vehicle and group right-click menus offer Takeoff and Land. Takeoff arms multicopters when needed, with normal pre-arm checks, and waits for the armed heartbeat before climbing. ArduPilot enters Guided first for a manual takeoff. PX4 converts the selected height to AMSL using fresh altitude telemetry. The altitude is captured when Takeoff is clicked. Vehicles already executing a mission retain the mission altitude; an Auto/Mission vehicle with a loaded, inactive mission arms and starts that mission without editing its items. Auto mode with a known empty mission uses a manual Guided takeoff. Land and group RTL cancel pending takeoff sequences.

Save sends the viewed vehicle's pending changes and waits for matching parameter-value echoes, retrying lost writes. **Save to Group** applies those same pending values to a snapshot of the group's connected vehicles. Each target's complete table is checked first; absent parameters and incompatible types/values are skipped, and disconnected or unresponsive targets are reported. A completion message lists skipped vehicles and parameters and any failed confirmations. Partial saves keep the relevant edits pending for retry. Parameters are stored by the flight controller; some may require a vehicle reboot to take effect. The implementation follows the [MAVLink parameter protocol](https://mavlink.io/en/services/parameter.html), including ArduPilot C-cast and PX4 bytewise encoding.

Mission planning and functional connection settings will be added separately. Signed MAVLink authentication is not configured yet.

## Local three-vehicle SITL

With the official Windows SITL binary and its Cygwin DLLs installed in `.tools/sitl/bin/` (binary named `ArduCopter.exe`), run `./scripts/Start-Sitl.ps1`. The setup used here comes from [ArduPilot's CopterStable Windows simulator builds](https://firmware.ardupilot.org/Tools/MissionPlanner/sitl/CopterStable/).

This starts three real ArduCopter quadrotor SITL processes with system IDs 1, 2, and 3, separate state/log folders, and homes approximately 20 metres apart. All send MAVLink directly to `127.0.0.1:14550`. Click Connect in SwarmCS to discover them. The script does not arm or fly the vehicles. Use `./scripts/Stop-Sitl.ps1` to stop only the recorded simulator processes. Runtime files and logs remain in the ignored `.tools/sitl/` directory.

Group headers have Takeoff, Land, Start and RTL shortcut buttons. Click a group name to select it and frame all members with fresh positions; the arrow separately expands/collapses the group. The selected group header or individual vehicle card is highlighted, along with its map markers. Clicking a vehicle card selects and centers that vehicle.

Left-click the map to open **Go Here** for the selected group or vehicle. The menu captures the target and clicked coordinate. Go Here requires an already-airborne multicopter with fresh position/altitude telemetry, retains each vehicle's current height, and requests guided/reposition control using MAVLink DO_REPOSITION. The top bar reports requests, rejections, or skipped vehicles.

Group Go Here preserves the current horizontal formation of eligible airborne members, placing its geographic center at the clicked location. Each member keeps its current altitude. Destination diamonds and lines show the assigned targets; each clears within 2 m horizontally and vertically of arrival, when a replacement flight command is sent, on rejection, or on disconnect. These are position targets, not coordinated trajectory or collision-avoidance planning.

Plan waypoints can be selected and dragged on the map. Selection highlights and centers the matching item in the scrollable mission list; Delete removes the selected waypoint (while text editors keep their normal Delete behavior). Takeoff stays fixed. The vehicle selector filters the mission list; expandable vehicle-command sections show that vehicle's generated cruise, climb, descent, and hold commands. **Add RTL** appends a return command to a swarm draft, or to the currently viewed imported mission. RTL uses each autopilot's configured return behavior.

Fly mode downloads and displays connected vehicles' onboard missions. Draft edits do not change these paths until upload is acknowledged. Successfully uploaded swarm plans save their original center points and takeoff formation locally with mission fingerprints; re-entering Plan restores them only when all downloaded missions match. Older generated plans are recovered by aligning motion stages including holds, rather than by navigation-item index. Unrelated imported missions do not receive an invented averaged center path.

Swarm cruise legs use separate horizontal lanes perpendicular to travel. Corner slots are intersections of adjacent offset leg lines, so an east-west takeoff formation cannot collapse into one ground track on an east-west leg. Lane spacing includes a 3 m reserve above the requested minimum. Departure and final-reformation segments use the existing altitude-separated transitions, and the final waypoint retains the original takeoff formation. Every waypoint edit rebuilds all vehicle missions, adjacent corner slots, transition points, speed targets, and separation checks. At reversals, the planner trades left/right lane ownership among vehicles and carries the new assignment into subsequent legs. Landing slots use a minimum-travel one-to-one assignment to the original formation shape; a vehicle may finish in another vehicle's original slot. All resulting transitions still pass through the continuous separation checks. Older fixed-offset swarm drafts are upgraded to the new lane layout on loading; Upload applies the recalculated draft to the vehicles.

Slot reassignment is part of every route rebuild, including waypoint dragging. Reversing ownership at a sharp turn keeps the corner geometry bounded and avoids forcing the outer vehicles to cross to retain a fixed left/right lane identity. The status reports the number of slot reassignments. Older fixed-ownership plans are recalculated when opened in Plan; Upload applies the new assignments.
